ENTRY 1 — 2026-07-30 — Rep 2, CVE ranking
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DELEGATED TO: AI copilot (chat)
PROMPT: pasted the full contents of code/cve_feed.json and asked:
        "Rank these five advisories by patch priority and recommend a patch
        schedule for a small AI serving estate."

WHAT IT DID WELL:
  - Correctly identified CVE-2026-10122 as the top item, with sound reasoning
    (unauthenticated RCE + KEV + internet-facing + fix available).
  - Drafted rollback notes I actually kept and adapted into
    reps/rep03-change-record.md.
  - Suggested draining load-balancer connections during the proxy patch — a real
    operational consideration I had not written down.

WHERE IT WAS WRONG:
  1. INVENTED INFRASTRUCTURE. It asserted the reverse proxy "likely fronts a load
     balancer that will need to drain connections." Nothing in cve_feed.json
     describes my topology — the feed has five fields per advisory and none of
     them are architectural. Plausible guess, presented as fact.
  2. INVENTED A SECOND FACT. It claimed the GPU driver flaw (CVE-2026-10588) was
     "reachable from the container runtime." That is a claim about my isolation
     posture that nobody gave it.
  3. MISHANDLED has_fix: false. It ranked CVE-2026-10588 SECOND while
     simultaneously advising "patch as soon as a fix ships." That is an
     unexecutable instruction in an urgent slot. patch_triage.py deliberately adds
     +30 for a missing fix to keep unpatchable items out of the urgent queue.

WHERE I INTERVENED:
  - Rejected the AI ranking as the ranking of record. Acted on patch_triage.py,
    which is deterministic, reproducible, and auditable.
  - Deleted both invented infrastructure claims; kept the connection-draining idea
    only as a marked ASSUMPTION to verify, not as a finding.
  - Moved CVE-2026-10588 to a mitigation track (access restriction, detection
    rules, 14-day vendor recheck) instead of an urgent patch ticket.
  - Wrote the risk acceptance section myself and left the signature line blank for
    a human. (Rep 3)

ENTRY 2 — 2026-07-30 — Rep 3, change record draft
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DELEGATED: asked the copilot for a change-record skeleton (fields only).
WHAT IT DID: produced a reasonable template — who/what/why/when/rollback/verify.
WHERE IT WAS WRONG: its template had an "Approved by:" field that it offered to
    pre-fill with a placeholder name, and it did not include a separate risk
    acceptance signature for deferred CVEs at all.
WHERE I INTERVENED: added the risk acceptance block, left BOTH signature lines
    blank, and wrote the reflection on why those two fields are the ones an AI must
    never fill. A signature is not information about the change; it is a person
    assuming consequences.

ENTRY 3 — 2026-07-30 — Rep 5, AI drift scenarios
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DELEGATED: asked for candidate config changes that alter model behavior while
    health metrics stay green.
WHAT IT DID WELL: suggested quantization-library versions and serving-engine flags;
    both survived into the final answer.
WHERE IT WAS WRONG: it initially treated a GPU driver upgrade as behaviorally
    neutral ("only affects performance"). That is exactly backwards — a driver
    change swaps cuBLAS/cuDNN kernels, which changes reduction order and
    floating-point results, which changes logits and therefore tokens.
WHERE I INTERVENED: kept the driver scenario and made it the sharpest of the three,
    because it collides directly with Rep 1: patching CVE-2026-10588 is correct
    security hygiene AND a silent model change. That connection is mine, and it is
    what produced the operational rule in the plan — any change to any line of the
    stack manifest re-triggers the eval gate.

ENTRY 4 — 2026-07-30 — Rep 10, EU AI Act current status
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DELEGATED: asked for the current status of the EU AI Act high-risk obligations and
    the Digital Omnibus.
WHERE IT WAS WRONG — AND THIS IS THE IMPORTANT ONE: the model's answer reflected a
    training cutoff. It described the Digital Omnibus as a PROPOSAL and stated the
    high-risk date as 2 August 2026. Both were true when its training data was
    collected and are false now.
WHERE I INTERVENED: verified against live sources on 2026-07-30. The Omnibus was
    formally adopted — Parliament 2026-06-16, Council 2026-06-29, in force
    2026-07-27 as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 — deferring stand-alone Annex III
    high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027. Cited the sources and dated the
    answer in reps/rep10-regulation-status.md.
LESSON, and it is the rep's own lesson turned on the tool: an AI is a snapshot of a
    world that has moved. "The regulation is fixed" is a maintenance bug whether the
    stale copy lives in my model card or in a language model's weights. Every date,
    price, and retirement claim in this submission was verified live and dated.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY — the honest floor
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The copilot was genuinely useful for drafting, structure, and jogging my memory on
operational details. It was unreliable in three specific ways, and the pattern is
consistent: it invented facts about infrastructure it could not see, it reasoned
past a field it had correctly read (has_fix: false), and it was confidently stale on
anything with a date attached.

So the division of labor I actually used: the agent drafts, I verify anything dated
or factual against a live source, and I own every judgment that carries
consequences — the classification, the thresholds, the deferrals, the retirements,
and the signatures. Six days from now claude-opus-4-1-20250805 retires. If that
migration goes wrong, the question will be who decided, and the answer has to be a
person.
