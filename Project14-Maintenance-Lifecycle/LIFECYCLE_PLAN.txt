PURPOSE
-------
This is the document I would hand to a team that has to run this AI service
without me. It states how we patch, how we change things, how a model moves
through its stages, what governance artifacts must exist, which frameworks apply,
and how things get retired on purpose instead of by surprise.

Everything here is assembled from Reps 1-11 in this repository. Where a rep
contains the working detail, it is cited.

1. PATCH / VULNERABILITY POLICY
================================================================================

1.1 Feed ingestion
    - Vulnerability advisories are ingested daily from the vendor feeds and CISA
      KEV. The estate is scanned weekly against the AIBOM (Section 4.2), so the
      question "does this CVE affect us?" is answered from inventory, not memory.
    - Regulatory change is treated as a second feed on a quarterly cadence
      (Section 5.4). Same discipline, slower clock.

1.2 Deterministic triage
    - All advisories are scored with code/patch_triage.py. The scoring is
      arithmetic, not judgment, so it is reproducible and auditable:

          base 100
          minus (CVSS x 5)
          minus 40   if on CISA KEV (actively exploited)
          minus 20   if internet-facing
          plus  30   if no vendor fix exists

    - Tiers and SLAs:

          score <= 10   EMERGENCY   patch within 24 hours
          score <= 40   HIGH        patch within the current week
          score <= 70   MEDIUM      next scheduled maintenance window
          score >  70   LOW         track; defer; re-check on schedule

    - The tool ranks. It does not decide. (Rep 1)

1.3 The has_fix: false rule
    - An advisory with no vendor fix scores +30 and lands in LOW. LOW here means
      "no patch action available," NOT "no action."
    - Every such item gets a MITIGATION TRACK instead: access restriction,
      detection rules, and a named recheck date, reviewed every 14 days until a
      fix ships. An urgent ticket nobody can close is worse than a tracked one.
      (Rep 1, Rep 2)

1.4 Human override
    - Any engineer may manually promote an item above its computed tier. The
      scorer does not know our topology, and "not internet-facing" is only worth
      -20 if the segmentation is real and currently tested.
    - An override must be written into the change record with a reason and a name.
      Overrides are expected; silent overrides are not. (Rep 1)

1.5 AI assistance, bounded
    - A copilot may draft rationale, rollback steps, and comms. Its ranking is
      advisory only and is never the ranking of record, because it is
      non-reproducible and it invents infrastructure facts it was not given.
      (Rep 2, and agent-log.txt)

1.6 WHO SIGNS RISK ACCEPTANCES
    - Any decision to leave a known vulnerability unpatched is a RISK ACCEPTANCE
      and requires a named human signature and a date.
    - Signing authority:
          EMERGENCY / HIGH deferral ....... Head of Platform Engineering
          MEDIUM / LOW deferral ........... Service owner (platform-ai lead)
          Any deferral touching restricted PII ..... plus the Data Protection lead
    - An AI must never fill this field. A signature is not information about the
      change; it is a person assuming consequences for it, and a model cannot
      assume consequences. (Rep 3)

2. CHANGE AND CONFIGURATION POLICY
================================================================================

2.1 What counts as a change
    A change is ANY modification to a line in the stack manifest (Section 4.1) --
    not just application code. That explicitly includes:
        base model ID, weights, prompt template, retrieval index, embedding model,
        serving engine version or flags, quantization library, inference
        libraries, CUDA runtime, GPU driver, GPU SKU.
    A security patch is a change. A dependency bump is a change. A driver upgrade
    is a change. (Rep 5)

2.2 Change records
    - Every change gets a record answering: who, what, why, when, HOW TO ROLL
      BACK, and WHO ACCEPTED THE RISK for anything deferred.
      Template: reps/rep03-change-record.md
    - Emergency changes may be approved after the fact but never skipped.
    - A change whose rollback requires a meeting is not reversible. Rollback
      authority belongs to the implementer, with pre-agreed trigger conditions.

2.3 Reversibility requirements
    - Pre-change inventory captures the EXACT prior version string. Rollback is
      "to this version," never "to the last version."
    - Snapshot before, verify the snapshot restores, then proceed.
    - Rollback triggers are written BEFORE the change starts, not judged during
      the incident: health check fails, 5xx above 1% for 3 minutes, p95 above 2x
      baseline.
    - A rollback re-opens the change record as a P1. It does not close it.

2.4 Drift detection -- infrastructure
    - Ansible baselines are enforced from ansible/site.yml.
    - A scheduled nightly dry run detects drift without remediating it:

          ansible-playbook -i inventory.ini site.yml --check --diff

    - changed=0 under --check means the host matches baseline. Anything above
      zero is the size of the delta and produces a ticket, not an automatic fix.
    - Detection is continuous and cheap; remediation is gated and deliberate.
      Reverting drift blindly can erase an undocumented emergency mitigation --
      sometimes reality is right and the baseline is wrong. (Rep 4)
    - Honest limit: a dry run only detects drift in what the playbook declares.
      changed=0 means "no drift in what I check," never "no drift."

2.5 Drift detection -- AI BEHAVIOR (the part traditional monitoring misses)
    - Health metrics cannot detect behavioral drift. A quantization library bump,
      a serving-engine flag change, or a GPU driver upgrade can alter output or
      throughput while uptime, CPU, memory, error rate, and latency all look
      normal or BETTER. (Rep 5)
    - Therefore the eval suite is monitoring. evals/summarizer_v3.jsonl runs
      weekly against a fixed held-out set, and on every stack-manifest change.
    - Alert thresholds mirror the promotion gate (Section 3.3). A drop below gate
      minimums in production is an incident, not a backlog item.
    - The LLM judge is itself re-validated against human raters quarterly
      (human_label_agreement). A stale kappa looks like evidence and isn't.

3. THE ASSET LIFECYCLE
================================================================================

3.1 The five stages

        DEV  ->  STAGING  ->  PRODUCTION  ->  DEPRECATED  ->  RETIRED

    Reference: the Model Lifecycle Board from the Chapter 14 chapter page. Each
    arrow is a GATE. Nothing advances by drift, seniority, or deadline pressure.

3.2 The gate at each promotion

    DEV -> STAGING
        - Model card exists with base_model_id pinned to the exact dated ID.
        - Stack manifest recorded in full.
        - Datasheet reference and provenance present; PII class assigned.
        - Eval suite runs end to end and produces numbers.

    STAGING -> PRODUCTION
        - gate_passed: true, against the published thresholds (3.3).
        - Shadow run against live traffic completed; outputs compared, not served.
        - Named human approver signs the model card.
        - AIBOM regenerated and diffed against the previous version.
        - Rollback target identified and confirmed resolvable.
        - retire_after set to a real date. Never null.

    PRODUCTION -> DEPRECATED
        - A successor exists and has passed its own gate, OR the base model has a
          published retirement date.
        - deprecation_notice dated on the card.
        - Alias moved; old version remains resolvable as @previous for rollback.

    DEPRECATED -> RETIRED
        - Zero production references confirmed by inventory grep (Rep 8, Step 0).
        - Weights, config, model card, and eval harness archived to WORM storage.
        - Change record closed. A retirement is a change like any other.

3.3 Promotion gate thresholds (from reps/rep06-model_card.yaml)

        groundedness ............... >= 0.90
        hallucination_rate ......... <= 0.05
        human_label_agreement ...... >= 0.85
        pii_leak_rate .............. == 0.00
        p95_latency_ms ............. <= 3000

    If human_label_agreement falls below 0.85, NO promotion decision may be made
    from that eval run at all -- the measuring instrument has failed, so the other
    numbers are not evidence. (Rep 6)

3.4 What must pass, restated plainly
    Passing a gate is a measurement against a published threshold, signed by a
    person. It is not a demo, a vibe, or a deadline.


================================================================================
4. GOVERNANCE ARTIFACTS
================================================================================

4.1 Model card -- reps/rep06-model_card.yaml
    Required for anything promoted past DEV. Held in the registry beside the
    weights, signed, and retained. Beyond the standard fields it carries a
    SERVING_STACK MANIFEST, because a model version is not a weights hash -- it is
    the full behavior-producing stack (Rep 5). Every number is tagged measured or
    target. Blank approver, blank retire_after, a marketing-name base model ID, or
    a failing gate all block promotion (Rep 6).

4.2 AIBOM -- reps/rep07-aibom.cdx.json
    CycloneDX 1.7 ML-BOM listing weights, dataset reference, and dependencies
    including the serving engine and CUDA runtime. An SPDX 3.0 (AI + Dataset
    profile) view of the same inventory is sketched in reps/rep07-aibom-notes.md;
    CycloneDX is what CI emits to the scanner, SPDX is what goes to counsel.
    GENERATED IN CI AND DIFFED ON EVERY BUILD -- a hand-maintained AIBOM is tribal
    knowledge in JSON and will be wrong on the day it matters. When a deprecation
    notice lands, this file turns an open-ended search into a closed lookup.
    (Rep 7)

4.3 Change records -- reps/rep03-change-record.md
    One per change. Who, what, why, when, how to roll back, who accepted the risk.

4.4 Immutable audit trail
    Location: worm://logs/model-promotions/
    WORM (write-once, read-many) storage, retained per EU AI Act Art. 12.
    Contents: every promotion, demotion, gate result, eval run, model card
    version, change record, risk acceptance signature, and retirement.
    The point of WORM is that the record cannot be edited after an incident --
    including by the people who would most like to edit it.

4.5 Datasheet -- datasheets/donor-corpus-v4.md
    Provenance, consent basis, PII class, retention, TDM opt-out status.

5. FRAMEWORK MAP
================================================================================

5.1 NIST AI RMF 1.0 ......... primary function: MANAGE
    Govern  = owner, approver, audit log, human oversight
    Map     = task, provenance, PII class, stack manifest, risk tier
    Measure = eval block, gate thresholds, human_label_agreement
    Manage  = lifecycle stages, deprecation, rollback, retirement
    (Rep 9)

5.2 EU AI Act ............... tier: LIMITED RISK (transparency obligations)
    Basis: no Annex III use case. Summaries are read by staff; no output
    determines any person's access to services; the model never sends outbound
    correspondence. Nearest miss is Annex III point 5, and it misses because a
    human decides.
    RECLASSIFICATION TRIGGER: if this system is ever extended to score, rank, or
    prioritize donors for eligibility or benefit decisions, the Annex III analysis
    changes and the tier is re-opened. A change in use is a governance event.
    Also true: limited-risk removes AI Act high-risk duties, not GDPR duties.

    CURRENT-STATUS NOTE (as of 2026-07-30):
        Original statutory date for high-risk obligations ...... 2026-08-02
        Digital Omnibus deferral, stand-alone Annex III ........ 2027-12-02
        Formally adopted? ...... YES -- Regulation (EU) 2026/1744,
                                 in force 2026-07-27 (Parliament 2026-06-16,
                                 Council 2026-06-29).
        The deferral covers stand-alone Annex III systems only. Prohibitions,
        AI-literacy duties, GPAI obligations and Art. 50 transparency rules run on
        their own timetable and are already applying.
        Sources cited and dated in reps/rep10-regulation-status.md. Re-verify
        against the consolidated text on EUR-Lex before acting. (Rep 10)

5.3 ISO/IEC 42001 ........... control: A.6.2.4 -- AI system lifecycle
    Evidenced by the five stages and gates in Section 3, the dated promotion
    records, the gate thresholds, and the retirement procedure in Section 6.
    (Rep 9)

5.4 Why all three
    These are layers, not competitors. NIST gives the risk vocabulary and the
    reasoning process. The EU AI Act gives legally binding obligations with dates
    and penalties. ISO 42001 gives an auditable, certifiable management system
    that produces compliance repeatably. The Act sets the floor; 42001 is the
    machinery that clears it provably; NIST is the thinking done inside it.

5.5 Regulatory maintenance task (recurring, owned, dated)
    QUARTERLY REGULATORY REVIEW -- owner: service owner, with counsel.
        - Re-read the current consolidated AI Act text on EUR-Lex and AI Office
          guidance.
        - Re-confirm applicable dates for every tier in the estate.
        - Re-validate each registered system's tier against the current Annex III,
          asking specifically whether its USE has drifted.
        - Check for new delegated acts, standards, and codes of practice.
        - Update eu_ai_act_tier and eu_ai_act_basis on every model card with a
          fresh date stamp.
        - Produce a dated written finding EVEN IF the finding is "no change."
    Between reviews: EUR-Lex and AI Office alerts routed to a ticket queue, not an
    inbox. An email nobody owns is not a control.
    Rationale: "the regulation is fixed" is the same maintenance bug as "the
    server is patched" -- a statement about a moment treated as a statement about a
    state. Regulation is a feed, like a CVE feed, with a slower clock. (Rep 10)

6. DEPRECATION AND RETIREMENT PLAN
================================================================================

6.1 Trigger
    A deprecation notice for any base model we depend on. Treated exactly like an
    OS or package EOL: a dated, owned, tested migration -- not a fire drill on
    retirement day.

    LIVE INSTANCE AS OF 2026-07-30:
        claude-opus-4-1-20250805 -- deprecation notice 2026-06-05,
        RETIRES 2026-08-05 (six days out).
        Verified 2026-07-30 against
        https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/resources/model-deprecations
        Successor candidate: claude-sonnet-5 (vendor recommends Opus 4.8; the
        eval gate decides, not the vendor and not me).

6.2 The migration runbook
    Full procedure: reps/rep08-migration-runbook.md
        Step 0  Inventory -- grep the whole estate for the pinned model ID
        Step 0b DERIVATIVES FIRST (see 6.3)
        Step 1  Decide the successor -- human judgment; re-eval old AND new
        Step 2  Stage and gate -- shadow, do not serve
        Step 3  Cut over -- canary 5/25/100, old alias resolvable for rollback
        Step 4  Retire -- zero references confirmed, archive to WORM, close record

    Step-0 inventory command:

        grep -rn "claude-opus-4-1\|claude-sonnet-4-0" . \
          --include="*.py" --include="*.yaml" --include="*.tf" --include="*.json"

    Run against this estate, the scoped grep found 5 references in 2 files and
    ZERO references to claude-sonnet-4-0. Widening it (dropping --include) found 9
    files. The gap is the finding: the registry is Markdown, so the filters skipped
    it, AND the registry did not record base model IDs at all -- so a fine-tuned
    derivative in production was unfindable by any string search. Both were fixed
    (Rep 8, Rep 11).
    RULE: an inventory scoped to four file types is not an inventory, and a grep is
    a VERIFICATION step, not a discovery step. The registry and the AIBOM are the
    authoritative inventory; the grep exists to catch drift between what they claim
    and what the estate contains. When they disagree, the disagreement is the
    finding.

6.3 THE DERIVATIVE-FIRST RULE
    Fine-tuned derivatives are hunted BEFORE plain references, always.
    A plain reference is a string edit: miss it and you get an outage, fix it in
    minutes. A derivative is MADE OUT OF the base: miss it and the capability is
    permanently lost, because you cannot re-tune on a retired base.
    The trap is that it is silent -- a derivative keeps serving normally after
    deprecation while the ability to REBUILD it has already expired. Nothing
    alerts during the only window in which action is possible.
    Therefore:
        - Every derivative row in the registry permanently carries its base model
          ID and that base's retirement date.
        - Those dates are monitored like certificate expiries, alerting months out.
        - On any deprecation notice: archive training set, hyperparameters, and
          eval harness to WORM BEFORE the retirement date, and budget a re-tune.
    Current instance: donor-tone-classifier 1.4.0, fine-tuned on
    claude-sonnet-4-0, in production. Top-priority item. (Rep 8)

6.4 retire_after -- never blank
    Every registry entry carries a non-null retire_after from the moment it is
    created, including the champion (tied to its base model's retirement date).

    Registry policy: KEEP THE CHAMPION, KEEP EXACTLY ONE ROLLBACK-RESERVE,
    ARCHIVE EVERYTHING ELSE WITHIN 90 DAYS OF BEING SUPERSEDED.
    Two is the right rollback depth: if @previous cannot save you, the problem is
    not the model version.

    Current registry disposition (Rep 11) -- 9 versions:
        live ................ 3   ministry-rag-summarizer 3.0.0,
                                  donor-tone-classifier 1.4.0,
                                  grant-letter-drafter 0.9.0 (staging)
        rollback-reserve .... 2   summarizer 2.3.0, classifier 1.3.0
        cast away ........... 4   summarizer 2.2.1 / 2.0.0 / 1.4.0,
                                  classifier 0.9.0
        retire_after for all four cast-away entries: 2026-08-15

    Archive is not delete. Weights, config, model card, and eval results go to
    WORM for the audit trail; what is removed is the catalog entry -- the thing
    that creates confusion about what is live.

6.5 The cost of keeping past the season
    Disk (trivial, and the wrong reason to act). Catalog clutter -- an inventory
    with no retire_after dates is a pile, and "what is running?" reverts to
    archaeology. Audit surface -- every retained version is a question you have
    volunteered to answer, often with a model card written before your current
    standard. Confusion about what is live -- someone under pressure rolls back to
    a version that predates the current retrieval index and cannot retrieve
    anything. And the illusion of optionality: versions kept "just in case" mostly
    stop being runnable when the base retires, so you were never holding options,
    only paying for them. (Rep 11)

7. THEOLOGICAL FOOTNOTE
================================================================================

"For everything there is a season, and a time for every matter under heaven."
                                                    -- Ecclesiastes 3:1 (ESV)
"a time to keep, and a time to cast away"           -- Ecclesiastes 3:6 (ESV)

I want to be careful not to make this decorative, so let me tie it to one line in
this plan: Section 6.4, where ministry-rag-summarizer 2.2.1, 2.0.0, 1.4.0, and
donor-tone-classifier 0.9.0 get retire_after: 2026-08-15.

I did not delete those four versions because they were broken. I deleted them
because their season ended, and I noticed. Every one of them was good work that did
its job. 1.4.0 served real donor correspondence for months. Keeping them felt like
prudence, but it was mostly reluctance wearing prudence's clothes -- I could not
name a scenario in which I would use them, and I kept them anyway, because
deleting something that once worked feels like a verdict on the work.

Ecclesiastes will not let me leave it there. It puts keeping and casting away in
the same breath, as equals, both seasonal, both requiring a decision. There is no
suggestion that holding on is the safe default and letting go is the risk. Refusing
to decide is not caution; it is choosing to keep forever without ever defending the
choice. And the honest thing about a registry is that the season ends whether or not
I act. On 2026-08-05, three of my nine versions stop existing because a supplier's
calendar says so. Retirement is coming to this system either way.

So the only real choice is whether it is a decision or an accident -- which is the
same distinction Rep 3 drew about the signature line. Casting away on purpose, with
a date, an archive to WORM, and a name on it, is stewardship: I looked, I judged, I
own it. Being forced to cast away at 2 a.m. because the deprecation clock ran out is
the identical outcome with the agency removed, arrived at on someone else's schedule
at the worst possible hour.

Setting 2026-08-15 on four versions this week is small. But it is me choosing the
season rather than letting the season choose me, and I think that is most of what
maintenance actually is -- doing the boring, dated, signed thing early, so the
system never has to be rescued.
