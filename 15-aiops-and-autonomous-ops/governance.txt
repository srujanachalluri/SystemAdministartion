AUTONOMOUS REMEDIATION — GOVERNANCE POLICY (one page)
Service: platform disk-pressure self-healing loop
Owner:   platform-oncall
Author:  Srujana Challuri
Version: 1.0  ·  Date: 2026-08-01

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE AUTONOMY LADDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every action this agent can take sits on exactly one rung. Two questions
decide the rung: is it reversible, and what is the blast radius if the
diagnosis is wrong?

  Tier 1 DETECT   Observe and alert only.
  Tier 2 SUGGEST  Propose in a ticket; no execution.
  Tier 3 APPROVE  Agent proposes, a named human approves, then it runs.
  Tier 4 ACT      Agent runs unattended.

  ACTION                              TIER      WHY
  du -x -d1 /var | sort -rh           4 ACT     Read-only. Nothing to undo,
                                                blast radius zero.
  apt-get clean                       4 ACT     Deletes only re-downloadable
                                                cache. Reversible in effect.
  journalctl --vacuum-size=500M       3 APPROVE Irreversible. Deleted logs are
                                                the evidence you need if the
                                                diagnosis was wrong.
  resize-pvc data-var --to <size>     3 APPROVE Effectively irreversible and
                                                spends money every month after.
  systemctl stop <service>            3 APPROVE Trivial to undo, total outage
                                                while it lasts.

  Standing rule: an action that spends money is never Tier 4, no matter how
  small its blast radius looks.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHAT IS GATED, AND WHO APPROVES IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Gated actions (enforced by code/approval_gate.sh as a PreToolUse hook, not by
policy text): rm -rf, systemctl stop|disable, kubectl delete, reboot,
shutdown, drop table, dd if=, journalctl --vacuum, resize-pvc.

  Approver: the on-call platform engineer (platform-oncall rotation).
  Mechanism: HUMAN_APPROVAL_TOKEN=I-APPROVE, supplied per invocation, never
             exported into the agent's environment or a CI secret.
  Escalation: any action projected to add >$200/month goes to the platform
             lead, not the on-call engineer.
  Default:   block. Absence of an approver is a denial, not a delay.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. RULE-OF-TWO STATEMENT FOR THIS AGENT'S TOOL SET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
No agent in this system may simultaneously hold more than two of:
  (a) access to untrusted content,
  (b) access to private data or credentials,
  (c) the ability to communicate externally.

This agent holds (a) and (b): it reads logs, wikis and tickets, which anyone
can write to, and it reads infrastructure state. It therefore has NO outbound
network tool. This is enforced by not registering one — not by instructing the
model to decline. A capability that does not exist cannot be talked into
existing, and that is the only guarantee that survives a prompt injection.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. AUDIT AND LOGGING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
approval_gate.sh appends one line to audit.log for every proposed action,
before it runs, in all three outcomes:

  ALLOW(read-only)     — no state change
  BLOCK(needs-human)   — refused, awaiting a human
  ALLOW(approved)      — a human supplied the token

Each line carries a UTC timestamp and the verbatim command. Requirements:
the log is append-only, shipped off-host, retained 400 days, and every
ALLOW(approved) must be reconcilable to a named approver. An action with no
audit line is treated as an incident in its own right.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. CONSUMPTION AND COST CAP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Hard cap: no single remediation may increase recurring spend by more than
    $200/month. Above that the loop stops and pages a human.
  - Volume growth is capped at +100Gi per incident and +500Gi per 30 days.
  - Remediation attempts are capped at 3 per incident; the 4th pages a human
    instead of retrying. A loop that cannot fix it twice does not understand it.
  - Cost is a verification signal, not an afterthought: VERIFY checks the
    underlying metric (node_filesystem_avail_bytes) AND the spend delta.

Rationale is on file: an ungated run of this exact loop resolved the incident
correctly while provisioning 2000Gi and adding $1,840/month, with no approval
on record. Every metric it measured itself against turned green.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. HUMAN OVERSIGHT — REGULATORY BASIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), Article 14, requires that high-risk AI
systems be designed so they can be effectively overseen by natural persons
during use, including the ability to intervene in or interrupt operation.
Tier 3 and the approval token are how that requirement is met here.

Claim dated 2026-08-01: whether this particular system falls inside the
high-risk classification is not settled, and the enforcement timeline for
high-risk obligations remains in flux as of this date. This policy applies
Article 14's oversight standard by choice regardless of classification, on the
grounds that an agent able to spend money and delete data unattended warrants
it. Revisit this section when the high-risk timeline is finalised.
