0. WHAT GOVERNANCE IS, AT CRI
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A governance framework is not a paragraph that says "we take AI seriously." It is
a control set where every row has a NAMED HUMAN OWNER, a CONCRETE ARTIFACT, and a
GATE with a cadence. A row with no owner is not governed; it is hoped for.

Three pillars, layered — they are not competitors:
  NIST AI RMF 1.0    voluntary US framework: GOVERN / MAP / MEASURE / MANAGE.
                     CRI uses it as the operating rhythm.
  EU AI Act          binding EU law: risk tiers + GPAI regime. CRI has EU donors
                     and EU-resident staff, so it is in scope regardless of where
                     the servers sit. Timeline is volatile — see §4.
  ISO/IEC 42001      certifiable AI management system (AIMS). CRI's target for
                     external assurance; the control set below is built so that
                     certification is a documentation exercise, not a rebuild.

2. THE OPERATING RHYTHM (NIST AI RMF mapped to CRI's calendar)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  GOVERN   Standing: the control set above; the autonomy ladder; the refused-uses
           list in ai-strategy.txt §1. Accountable: Grace Adeyemi.
           Cadence: 90-day review meeting, 60 minutes, minuted, decisions logged.
  MAP      Every new AI use case is classified BEFORE build: data class, EU AI Act
           risk tier, affected persons, refusal check. Owner: Priya Raman.
           A use case with no risk-register entry does not get an environment.
  MEASURE  The six success measures in ai-strategy.txt §5, plus: retrieval
           precision on a held-out case-note question set, hallucination/refusal
           rate, and red-team findings open vs closed. Owner: Hannah Kim.
  MANAGE   Incident response, autonomy demotion (automatic, on any unexplained
           outcome), model deprecation, and the quarterly price/version re-verify.
           Owner: Peter Novak.

3. DECISION RIGHTS — WHO CAN SAY YES, AND WHO CAN SAY STOP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  New AI use case ...................... Priya Raman classifies; Grace Adeyemi approves
  Promotion up the autonomy ladder ..... Hannah Kim proposes; Grace Adeyemi signs
  Demotion down the ladder ............. AUTOMATIC on any unexplained outcome; no
                                         meeting required, no approval required
  Model version change ................. Daniel Okoro, via PR, two-person review
  Spend cap change ..................... Ruth Mensah + Grace Adeyemi jointly
  Access to RESTRICTED case notes ...... Miguel Santos, quarterly recertification
  STOP AUTHORITY (kill switch) ......... ANY of the nine. No permission needed.
                                         Owner of the mechanism: Priya Raman.
                                         Tested quarterly.

  The asymmetry is deliberate and it is the core governance stance: turning
  autonomy UP requires a named signature; turning it DOWN requires nothing at all.
  Anyone at CRI who thinks the machine is wrong can stop it, and will not be asked
  to justify that before the fact. They will be asked afterward, and "I was not
  sure" is an acceptable answer.

4. APPENDIX A — REGULATION MAPPING
    One autonomous remediation workflow -> EU AI Act Art. 14 + Art. 12 + NIST RMF
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORKFLOW MAPPED: A-9 clear_transient_disk_alert_by_rotating_logs
(automation-plan.txt, 'act' rung — the agent performs this WITHOUT a human.)

  Why this one: it is the workflow where CRI's automation is at its most
  autonomous, so it is where the regulatory obligations bind hardest. If the
  mapping holds here it holds everywhere below it.

  EU AI ACT ART. 14 — HUMAN OVERSIGHT
  Obligation (paraphrased): high-risk AI systems shall be designed so that they
  can be EFFECTIVELY OVERSEEN by natural persons, who must be able to understand
  the system's capacities and limits, remain aware of automation bias, correctly
  interpret its output, decide not to use it, and INTERVENE OR INTERRUPT via a
  stop button or equivalent.
    CRI's implementation, clause by clause:
      "understand capacities and limits"  -> the action's row in
          autonomy-ladder.yaml states tier, guardrails, blast radius, and
          rationale in plain language. Hannah Kim's onboarding requires her to
          be able to explain every 'act'-tier row without reading it.
      "aware of automation bias"          -> the 3-restarts-per-hour and
          allow-listed-paths guardrails exist precisely because a human watching
          a green dashboard stops watching. The limit fires without a human.
      "correctly interpret output"        -> every action writes intent BEFORE
          execution and outcome after, to the same WORM timeline, so the
          handover note reads as cause -> action -> result.
      "decide not to use it"              -> the action can be disabled per
          namespace by editing one YAML field; no code change, no redeploy.
      "intervene or interrupt"            -> the kill switch (automation-plan
          G-6): one documented command revokes every agent token and disables
          the ladder. ANY of the nine may pull it. Tested quarterly.
      "human oversight is assigned"       -> Art. 14 requires a natural person.
          For A-9 that person is Hannah Kim, named in the YAML, named here, and
          named in the incident runbook.

  EU AI ACT ART. 12 — RECORD-KEEPING / LOGGING
  Obligation (paraphrased): high-risk systems shall technically allow for the
  AUTOMATIC RECORDING OF EVENTS (logs) over the system's lifetime, appropriate to
  its purpose, enabling traceability.
    CRI's implementation:
      - Intent is written to a WORM (object-lock) bucket BEFORE the action runs.
        If the log write fails, THE ACTION DOES NOT RUN. Logging is a
        precondition, not a side effect.
      - Each record carries: timestamp (UTC), action name, ladder tier, the
        pinned model digest, the input telemetry that triggered it, the guardrail
        evaluations and their results, the outcome, and the rollback state.
      - Retention 7 years; no production identity, human or agent, holds a
        credential that can delete from that bucket (security-plan A-6).
      - Traceability test: for any action in the last 7 years, Priya Raman can
        reconstruct what the system saw, what it did, which model version did it,
        and who was accountable. That reconstruction is exercised in the
        twice-yearly tabletop, not assumed.

  NIST AI RMF FUNCTIONS
      GOVERN  : the ladder is a signed policy with an accountable executive and a
                90-day review; promotion is change-managed.
      MAP     : A-9 is classified in the risk register — infrastructure telemetry
                only, no personal data, bounded blast radius, reversible.
      MEASURE : tracked metrics — actions executed, guardrail trips, rollbacks
                fired, rollbacks that failed (target 0), and demotion events.
      MANAGE  : automatic demotion on any unexplained outcome; incident runbook
                covers "agent acted wrongly" as a named scenario.

  ISO/IEC 42001 LINKAGE
      The ladder + control set + 90-day review is the AIMS management cycle. The
      artifacts named in §1 are the evidence an auditor would sample.

5. APPENDIX B — WHAT GOVERNANCE DOES NOT COVER (stated on purpose)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - This framework does not make CRI compliant with any regulation. It makes CRI
    ready to demonstrate what it does. Compliance is a finding, not a claim.
  - It does not cover partner agencies' own AI use with data CRI shares with
    them. That gap is real, it is on the risk register, and closing it is a 2027
    contractual workstream. Owner: Grace Adeyemi.
  - It does not cover beneficiaries' understanding of AI's role in their case.
    CRI's position is that a beneficiary should never need to know, because AI is
    never the decider (ai-strategy R1). If that ever changes, this framework must
    be reopened before the change ships, not after.
