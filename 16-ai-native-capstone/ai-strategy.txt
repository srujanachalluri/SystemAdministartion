0. PURPOSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The board's mandate is two sentences:
  (a) Use AI to do far more with our nine people.
  (b) Never again put the people we serve, or the trust they place in us, at the
      mercy of a tool we did not govern.

This document answers two SEPARATE questions that are usually collapsed into one
and should not be:

  THREAD A - THE TOOL THREAD:     What does CRI use AI *for*?
                                  (AI as an assistant to our nine-person team)

  THREAD B - THE WORKLOAD THREAD: What AI workloads does CRI *run*?
                                  (AI as a system we operate, secure, and pay for)

A strong tool thread with an empty workload thread is a shopping list. A strong
workload thread with an empty tool thread is a lab experiment. CRI needs both.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THREAD A — WHAT CRI USES AI FOR (the tool thread)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Nine people support 600 staff across four continents. Every hour of toil we remove
is an hour returned to the mission. These are the uses, ranked by value per risk.

A1. SERVICE DESK DEFLECTION AND TRIAGE                   Owner: Peter Novak
    Assistant drafts first-response answers from our internal runbooks and
    knowledge base. Human sends. No auto-close, no auto-reset of credentials.
    Data class: INTERNAL. Expected effect: ~40% of L1 tickets drafted in <60s.

A2. AI-ASSISTED MONITORING / AIOps TRIAGE                Owner: Hannah Kim
    Alert correlation, noise suppression, and a plain-language "probable cause"
    note attached to each incident. Read-only against telemetry.
    Data class: INTERNAL. Expected effect: fewer 2 a.m. pages for known-noise.

A3. INFRASTRUCTURE-AS-CODE AND SCRIPT DRAFTING           Owner: Daniel Okoro
    Agent drafts Ansible/Terraform/Bash; a human reviews and merges via PR.
    Nothing an agent writes reaches production without a named reviewer.
    Data class: INTERNAL.

A4. GRANT AND DONOR REPORT DRAFTING                      Owner: Ruth Mensah
    Drafting narrative sections of donor reports from APPROVED, de-identified
    programme statistics. Never from raw case notes.
    Data class: CONFIDENTIAL (aggregate only).

A5. TRANSLATION AND FIELD COMMUNICATION SUPPORT          Owner: Aisha Bello
    Four continents, many working languages. Machine translation of operational
    and training material. Human review before anything is published to a
    beneficiary-facing channel.
    Data class: INTERNAL / PUBLIC.

A6. CASE-NOTE RETRIEVAL FOR AUTHORISED CASEWORKERS       Owner: Miguel Santos
    A retrieval-augmented assistant over beneficiary case notes, so a caseworker
    can ask "what support has this household already received?" instead of reading
    forty PDFs. THIS IS THE HIGHEST-RISK USE IN THE ORGANISATION and it is the
    reason the whole architecture exists. It is served ONLY by the self-hosted
    lane (see Thread B, W1). It is never sent to a third-party API.
    Data class: RESTRICTED (special-category personal data: names, locations,
    medical details of vulnerable people).

EXPLICITLY REFUSED USES (this list is as important as the list above):
  R1. No AI in beneficiary eligibility, selection, or aid-allocation decisions.
      An automated decision about who receives food or medical aid affects a
      person's access to essential services and would sit squarely in the EU AI
      Act's high-risk band. CRI accepts AI as a retrieval and drafting aid here,
      never as a decider.
  R2. No AI-generated content published in a beneficiary's voice or name.
  R3. No uploading of raw case notes, photographs of beneficiaries, or field
      security reports into any consumer chatbot. This is the exact failure that
      happened last year. It is now a technical control, not a policy hope
      (see security-plan.txt, control S-7: egress DLP + allow-listed endpoints).
  R4. No AI voice or video that could be mistaken for a real CRI staff member.
      CRI was nearly defrauded of US$240,000 by a deepfaked "CFO." We will not
      normalise synthetic likeness of our own people.

2. THREAD B — WHAT AI WORKLOADS CRI RUNS (the workload thread)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Three workloads. Two lanes. One control plane.

W1. CASE-NOTE RAG (self-hosted)                          Owner: Daniel Okoro
    Serving: vLLM behind KServe on the CRI Kubernetes cluster (see
    code/inference-platform.yaml). Weights shipped as OCI artifacts, signed and
    scanned in our own registry.
    Model tier: MID-SIZE OPEN-WEIGHT INSTRUCT MODEL, ~70B class, quantised to
    FP8/INT8, pinned by digest. Not "latest." Not the largest model available.
    Embeddings + vector index run in the same trust boundary as the documents.
    WHY SELF-HOST: data residency and non-retention for special-category data
    about vulnerable people. This is not a cost decision. It is a duty-of-care
    decision, and cost-analysis.txt shows what that duty costs us per month.

W2. STAFF PRODUCTIVITY ASSISTANT (API)                   Owner: Peter Novak
    Serving: a commercial API behind an OpenAI-compatible gateway, with zero-data-
    retention contractual terms and a per-day spend cap.
    Model tier: SMALL/FAST TIER for classification, routing, summarising short
    internal text; MID TIER escalation only when the small tier's confidence
    check fails. Frontier tier is not routinely used.
    WHY API: bursty, low-sensitivity, ~600 users. Buying capacity we would use
    for four hours a day is a bad use of donor money.

W3. AIOps / OPERATIONS AGENT (hybrid)                    Owner: Hannah Kim
    Reasoning over telemetry runs on the API lane (no beneficiary data present).
    Any action it proposes is gated by autonomy-ladder.yaml. See
    automation-plan.txt.
    Model tier: MID TIER. Tool access through MCP with an explicit, enumerated
    tool allow-list per rung.

MODEL-TIER POLICY (the "right model for the job" rule):
    Tier 0 - deterministic code:  If a regex, a SQL query, or a script does it,
                                  do not call a model. Most "AI wins" at CRI's
                                  scale are actually automation wins.
    Tier 1 - small/fast API:      classification, routing, extraction, short
                                  summaries. Default. Cheapest per call.
    Tier 2 - mid, self-hosted:    anything touching RESTRICTED beneficiary data.
    Tier 3 - mid/large API:       long-form drafting, code, complex reasoning on
                                  non-sensitive material. Requires a reason.
    Tier 4 - frontier:            exception only, approved by Grace Adeyemi,
                                  logged with justification.
    Every tier pins an explicit model ID and version. "latest" is banned org-wide
    because a silent model swap is an ungoverned change to a production system.

3. BUILD vs BUY vs SELF-HOST — THE STANCE, AND ITS DEFENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                        DECISION        WHY
  Case-note RAG         SELF-HOST       Residency + non-retention duty to
                                        vulnerable beneficiaries. Cost is the
                                        price of the duty, not the driver.
  Staff assistant       BUY (API)       Bursty, low sensitivity, no capacity to
                                        run GPUs for it. Spend-capped.
  AIOps agent           BUY the model,  Managed agent platforms are moving fast
                        BUILD the       and lock in hard. We buy inference and
                        guardrails      own the policy layer (autonomy ladder,
                                        approval gates, audit trail).
  Vector store          BUY managed,    Inside our residency boundary.
                        self-hosted     Portable index format.
  Observability         BUY, OTel-      One backbone, vendor-swappable.
                        native

  WE DO NOT: train or fine-tune foundation models. A nine-person team that
  fine-tunes a model has just adopted a second production system, a data pipeline,
  an evaluation harness, and a retraining schedule. We do retrieval instead, which
  we can inspect, correct, and delete. Deletion matters: a beneficiary who
  withdraws consent can be removed from an index today. They cannot be removed
  from a set of weights.

4. DURABLE-STANDARDS BET (the architecture must survive a rename)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CRI commits to INTERFACES, not brands:
  - OpenAI-compatible /v1/chat/completions  -> swap providers without a rewrite
  - OpenTelemetry                            -> one telemetry backbone
  - OCI artifacts for model weights          -> signing, scanning, promotion
  - MCP for agent-to-tool, A2A agent-to-agent
  - Open Inference Protocol for serving
  - CycloneDX ML-BOM for the AI bill of materials
Every product named in this document set is named as an IMPLEMENTATION of one of
these interfaces. If the vendor is acquired or renamed, the architecture stands.

  M5. Monthly AI spend vs cap                                 target: <=100%
  M6. Autonomy-ladder actions executed at 'act' tier with a
      rollback that did NOT fire correctly                    target: 0

  Any measure that cannot be produced from a dashboard on demand is deleted at
  the next review. We do not keep metrics we cannot see.
